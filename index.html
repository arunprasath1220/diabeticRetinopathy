Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

A screening, explainability and capacity-planning pipeline for fundus photographs, running entirely in the browser. Built for Smart India Hackathon problem statement SIH26038, sponsored by MathWorks.

Run it on an image Clinical reference

The problem

India has roughly 77 million diabetic adults, and about 18% of them develop diabetic retinopathy, a leading cause of preventable blindness. Early screening prevents around 90% of the resulting vision loss. The obstacle is not medicine but arithmetic: rural India has roughly one ophthalmologist per 100,000 people, so manual screening cannot reach the population that needs it.

Automated tools exist, but those deployed so far tend to be opaque, thinly validated, and brittle on the inconsistent images that low-cost portable cameras actually produce. This project aims at the opposite. Every step shows its working, and every step says plainly what it does not know.

The pipeline

An image passes through six stages. The two that point at disease do so by unrelated means, and are deliberately never merged into a single score. A grading step then applies the published ICDR rule to what was found, and raises a referral warning whenever the result should not stand alone.

Stages 2 and 4 both point at disease, by unrelated methods. Keeping them apart is deliberate: when they disagree, that disagreement is information, and averaging it away would hide it.

The core idea: telling a lesion from a blood vessel

This was the hardest part of the project, and the obvious answer is wrong. The instinct is to separate lesions from vessels by size, filtering away anything too small or too thin. That cannot work, because a microaneurysm is smaller than a blood vessel is wide. Any filter narrow enough to remove vessels removes every small lesion along with them. Building it that way was a real bug in this project, and it silently discarded the lesions that mattered most.

What actually separates them is elongation. A vessel keeps going; a lesion does not. So the image is probed along straight lines at twelve orientations. A vessel survives the probe aligned with the direction it runs. A round lesion is shorter than the probe in every direction, so every orientation catches it.

The same test excludes the macula without being told where the macula is. The response depends on how steeply intensity changes, not on how dark something is, so a lesion's step edge answers strongly while the macula's gradual darkening barely answers at all.

What is real, and what is not

The most common failing in tools of this kind is presenting every output with the same confidence, whatever produced it. Here is what each part of this app actually rests on.

OutputWhat it really is
Image quality scoresReal measurements from the pixels. The thresholds are prototype heuristics, not clinically validated cutoffs.
Disease classificationGenuine inference from a real MobileNet trained on APTOS 2019, running client-side. Two classes only, not a five-level ICDR grade.
Confidence figuresRaw softmax outputs. Not calibrated probabilities. A reading of 100% is not 100% certainty; it means the image sits far on one side of the decision boundary.
Grad-CAMReal gradients from the loaded model. Coarse by nature, so a region boundary means "somewhere around here", not an outline.
Lesion candidatesClassical morphology, no learning, independent of the classifier. Candidates, not findings. No accuracy is claimed, because none has been measured against ground truth.
Severity gradeThe published ICDR rule applied to unvalidated candidates. It is what the rule yields if those candidates were confirmed, which is a projection rather than a diagnosis. It cannot exclude severe or proliferative disease, because venous beading, IRMA and neovascularisation are all undetectable by this method.
Sensitivity and specificityNot claimed. No peer-reviewed validation exists for this exact model, so no figures are quoted.
Throughput simulationArithmetic on your own inputs, with model speed measured live on your device. A planning aid, not a clinical result.

This is a research and hackathon prototype. It is not a certified medical device, and nothing it produces is a diagnosis.
