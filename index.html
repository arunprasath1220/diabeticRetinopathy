Clinical reference

Findings graded by the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) severity scale. This section is fixed reference text, independent of any single image or model run.

ICDR severity scale

LevelGradeFindings
0No DRNo abnormalities.
1Mild NPDRMicroaneurysms only.
2Moderate NPDRMore than microaneurysms but less than severe NPDR (dot/blot hemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton wool spots present but limited). Referable threshold.
3Severe NPDR4:2:1 rule — >20 intraretinal hemorrhages in each of 4 quadrants, venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, or prominent IRMA in ≥1 quadrant; no proliferative signs yet.
4Proliferative DRNeovascularization and/or vitreous or preretinal hemorrhage.

Level 2 and above is referable — the patient should see an ophthalmologist.

Lesion vocabulary

Microaneurysms (MA)

Small, round, red dots; the earliest visible sign of DR; outpouchings of retinal capillary walls.

Dot and blot hemorrhages

Small, round hemorrhages in deeper retinal layers, larger than microaneurysms.

Flame-shaped hemorrhages

Hemorrhages in the superficial nerve fiber layer, following the curve of the nerve fibers.

Hard exudates

Sharply defined yellow-white lipid deposits, often ringing leaking microaneurysms.

Cotton wool spots (soft exudates)

Pale, fluffy-edged patches from localized nerve fiber layer infarcts.

Venous beading

Irregular, sausage-like venous dilation; a marker of more severe pre-proliferative disease.

Intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA)

Abnormal dilated capillary segments, a precursor to neovascularization.

Neovascularization (NVD/NVE)

New fragile vessel growth on/near the disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE); defines proliferative DR.

Vitreous or preretinal hemorrhage

Bleeding from neovascular vessels into the vitreous; a sight-threatening complication.

Step 3

Explainability — Grad-CAM

3a. Attention gradient with marked regions

Grad-CAM gradient (dark = low attention, light = high attention). Circled and numbered regions are the connected high-activation areas.

Computed from real gradients of the loaded model's output with respect to the last convolutional layer (conv_pw_13_relu), following the standard Grad-CAM formulation. Always computed for the "DR present" class score, so it shows evidence the model finds for disease even when its final prediction is "No DR". Marked regions are connected components of the gradient above 60% of peak activation, filtered to remove specks. They mark where the model looked, which is not the same as a confirmed lesion.

3b. Anatomical landmarks and retinal quadrants

Estimated landmarks with quadrant axes centred on the optic disc.

How these are estimated, and why that matters. The optic disc is located as the brightest large region after heavy blurring, which suppresses small bright lesions and specular reflections. The fovea is then placed roughly 2.5 disc diameters from the disc along the axis toward the image centre, and the macula is drawn as a circle around it — standard anatomical proportions, not a detection. Because the optic disc always lies nasal to the fovea in both eyes, the nasal/temporal axis is read directly off the disc position and needs no left/right eye information. Superior is taken as the top of the frame, per normal fundus photography convention. These are geometric estimates from a heuristic, not the output of a trained landmark detector, and they can be wrong on images with bright artefacts, heavy vignetting, or an off-centre disc. Use them for orientation, not measurement.

Step 4

Lesion candidate mask

This is classical image processing, not the neural network. The mask below is produced by morphology on the pixels — local background subtraction, then shape and contrast filtering. It is independent of the classifier in Step 2, it did not influence the grade, and the grade did not influence it. Every region is a candidate, not a confirmed lesion.

How each type is decided

Microaneurysm candidateSmall, round, isolated region darker than its local background. Separated from a hemorrhage by area alone, which is the weakest of these rules.
Dot/blot hemorrhage candidateSame darkness test, larger area, still compact rather than elongated.
Hard exudate candidateRegion brighter than local background with a sharp edge, measured as mean gradient magnitude across the region.
Cotton wool spot candidateBrighter than local background but with a soft, low-gradient edge.
Rejected as vesselDark regions that are elongated or poorly filled within their bounding box. Blood vessels are dark and linear, so this test removes them.
Rejected as optic discBright regions inside the estimated disc from Step 3b. The disc is the brightest structure in a normal retina and would otherwise dominate the bright mask.

What this method cannot do

These limits are structural, not tuning problems. Flame-shaped hemorrhages and neovascularization are largely invisible to it — both are elongated, so the vessel-rejection test discards them, which means the findings that define proliferative DR are exactly the ones this method misses. It cannot separate a true microaneurysm from dust on the lens, sensor noise, or a dark pigment spot, because at this resolution they are the same few dark pixels. Venous beading and IRMA are not detected at all, as both require judging vessel calibre along its length. The microaneurysm versus hemorrhage split rests on area alone and will misassign borderline sizes. Classical detectors of this kind are known for high false-positive rates on anything but clean images, and this one has not been validated against ground-truth lesion masks, so no sensitivity or specificity is claimed. It runs on the enhanced image, which raises contrast for detection but can also amplify noise into candidates. Treat the mask as a prompt for where to look, never as a finding.

A learned segmentation model — a U-Net trained on pixel-level lesion annotations such as IDRiD or DDR — is what would replace this. That is still not present in this prototype, and this morphological stand-in is not a substitute for it.

Summary

Screening report

Classifier: MobileNet-based binary diabetic-retinopathy detector converted to TensorFlow.js by vbookshelf/Diabetic-Retinopathy-Analyzer, trained via the Kaggle kernel "DR - MobileNet Binary Classifier + TFJS Web App" on the APTOS 2019 Blindness Detection dataset. Weights are loaded at runtime from that public repository; no formal peer-reviewed sensitivity/specificity has been published for this exact model, so none is claimed here. This is a research/hackathon prototype, not a certified diagnostic device.
