Clinical reference

Findings graded by the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) severity scale. This section is fixed reference text, independent of any single image or model run.

ICDR severity scale

LevelGradeFindings
0No DRNo abnormalities.
1Mild NPDRMicroaneurysms only.
2Moderate NPDRMore than microaneurysms but less than severe NPDR (dot/blot hemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton wool spots present but limited). Referable threshold.
3Severe NPDR4:2:1 rule — >20 intraretinal hemorrhages in each of 4 quadrants, venous beading in ≥2 quadrants, or prominent IRMA in ≥1 quadrant; no proliferative signs yet.
4Proliferative DRNeovascularization and/or vitreous or preretinal hemorrhage.

Level 2 and above is referable — the patient should see an ophthalmologist.

Lesion vocabulary

Microaneurysms (MA)

Small, round, red dots; the earliest visible sign of DR; outpouchings of retinal capillary walls.

Dot and blot hemorrhages

Small, round hemorrhages in deeper retinal layers, larger than microaneurysms.

Flame-shaped hemorrhages

Hemorrhages in the superficial nerve fiber layer, following the curve of the nerve fibers.

Hard exudates

Sharply defined yellow-white lipid deposits, often ringing leaking microaneurysms.

Cotton wool spots (soft exudates)

Pale, fluffy-edged patches from localized nerve fiber layer infarcts.

Venous beading

Irregular, sausage-like venous dilation; a marker of more severe pre-proliferative disease.

Intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA)

Abnormal dilated capillary segments, a precursor to neovascularization.

Neovascularization (NVD/NVE)

New fragile vessel growth on/near the disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE); defines proliferative DR.

Vitreous or preretinal hemorrhage

Bleeding from neovascular vessels into the vitreous; a sight-threatening complication.

How the system reaches a conclusion

The pipeline has five stages. Each one is independent of the others in a way that matters: a later stage never adjusts an earlier one to make the answer look tidier, and the two that identify disease do so by completely different means and are never combined into a single score.

1. Image quality

Focus is measured as the variance of a Laplacian filter, brightness as the mean and spread of the greyscale histogram, and field of view as the fraction of the frame that is not black border. A frame that fails outright is rejected with the specific reason rather than screened anyway. A borderline frame is enhanced first, using local contrast equalisation, illumination flattening and light denoising.

2. Classification

A MobileNet trained on APTOS 2019 fundus images runs entirely in the browser and returns a genuine two-class result, no disease against disease present. It is not a five-level severity grader, and the app never pretends otherwise. Its confidence figures are raw softmax outputs, not calibrated probabilities, so a reading of 100% means the image sits far on one side of the model's decision boundary and nothing more.

3. Explainability

Grad-CAM shows where the network looked, computed from real gradients of its output with respect to an internal convolutional layer. It answers "what drove this prediction", not "where are the lesions", and the two questions have different answers. The optic disc draws strong attention from almost any fundus classifier because it is the most distinctive structure in the frame, so disc regions are shown but never counted as findings.

4. Lesion candidates

This stage is classical image processing with no machine learning in it at all, and it is independent of stage 2. It separates lesions from blood vessels by elongation rather than by size, which is the only property that actually distinguishes them: a microaneurysm is smaller than a vessel is wide, so no size threshold can tell them apart. Measuring along lines at twelve orientations, a vessel survives the test in the direction it runs while a round lesion is caught by every direction. The same property excludes the macula for free, since the response depends on how steeply intensity changes rather than on how dark something is.

Every region it reports is a candidate. Nothing here has been validated against ground-truth lesion masks, so no accuracy figure is claimed for it.

5. ICDR severity estimate

The ICDR scale is defined by which lesion types are present and, for severe disease, by how many hemorrhages appear in each quadrant. That is what stage 4 produces, so the published rule is applied to those counts directly. The rule is followed faithfully; the counts it is fed are not validated, so the grade is what the rule yields if those candidates were confirmed findings.

The ceiling matters more than the grade. Two of the three arms of the 4-2-1 rule are venous beading and IRMA, and proliferative disease is defined by neovascularisation. None of the three is detectable here, because all are elongated and the vessel test removes them by construction. So the estimate can reach severe NPDR only by counting hemorrhages, can never reach proliferative DR, and can never rule either of them out. A low grade here does not mean a low grade in the eye.

A red referral banner appears whenever the result should not stand on its own: at or above the referable threshold, when any lesion is seen at all, when landmarks fail, when image quality was borderline, when the candidate count suggests noise, when hemorrhage counts sit on the rule's boundary, or when the classifier and the lesion detector disagree with each other.

6. Throughput planning

A deliberately simple capacity model, not part of the AI at all. It takes acquisition rate, bandwidth, measured model speed and reviewer capacity, and reports which stage is the bottleneck. A production version of this would be a Simulink model.

What the lesion detector cannot do

These limits are structural rather than tuning problems. Flame-shaped hemorrhages and neovascularisation are largely invisible to it, because both are elongated and the vessel test discards them, which means the findings that define proliferative disease are exactly the ones it misses. Venous beading and intraretinal microvascular abnormalities are not detected at all, as both require judging vessel calibre along its length. The split between microaneurysm and hemorrhage rests on area alone. A trained segmentation model would replace all of it, and the project README explains what that would take.
